# LLM: Do's and Don'ts

We interact with LLMs more than ever before. They are in our workflows,
our creative processes, and our daily routines.

That is useful. But there is a quiet danger: the shift from **augmenting
our humanity to replacing it**.

My rule is simple:

> **Use AI to think better, not to think less.**

## What LLMs are genuinely good at

LLMs are excellent tools for work where speed, iteration, and
information processing matter:

-   Searching through large amounts of text
-   Summarizing documents
-   Explaining difficult concepts
-   Brainstorming
-   Rubber-ducking code
-   Finding bugs and suggesting approaches
-   Drafting and rewriting
-   Formatting repetitive work
-   Turning messy thoughts into clearer ones

They are particularly good when the human remains in the loop.

Ask the model to give you possibilities. Ask it to challenge your
assumptions. Ask it to explain something five different ways.

Then **think**.

## What I don't want AI to replace

There are places where the tool becomes dangerous precisely because it
is so convenient.

### Don't replace real relationships

ChatGPT can simulate conversation. It cannot replace friends, family,
partners, communities, or the friction of interacting with actual human
beings.

Real relationships contain empathy, disagreement, history,
responsibility, and accountability.

Replacing them with an algorithm is not an efficiency improvement.

It is dehumanization.

### Don't make AI your therapist or decision-maker

AI can help organize your thoughts. It can help you identify patterns or
prepare questions for a professional.

But I don't want an LLM deciding:

-   Who I should marry
-   Whether I should quit my job
-   Whether I should move countries
-   What major life choice I should make

A better question is:

> **"Help me list the options, risks, assumptions, and things I may have
> overlooked."**

Then I make the decision.

### Don't confuse confidence with truth

LLMs produce fluent language. Fluency is not evidence.

Verify important:

-   Numbers
-   Laws
-   Medical information
-   Historical claims
-   Quotes
-   Citations
-   Technical behavior

A confident answer can still be completely wrong.

## The bigger problem: centralization

The discussion about AI often stops at individual usage.

I think there is a larger systems problem.

Mass LLM adoption means massive centralized inference infrastructure,
enormous resource consumption, and increasing dependence on a small
number of companies.

There are also questions about who controls knowledge, models,
infrastructure, and access.

That is why decentralization matters to me.

True progress should not mean outsourcing more and more of our cognition
to a remote service until we can no longer function without it.

It should mean **more agency**.

## Local AI is more than a technical hobby

I strongly encourage experimenting with recent local, open-weight models
such as **Qwen 3.8 27B** and **Muse Glimmer** families, probably the strongest consumer-grade LLM there is that can run on a simple 16-24GB VRAM RTX GPUs or an apple M4 machines.

Running models locally can mean:

-   Less dependence on a cloud provider
-   Greater privacy
-   More control
-   Offline capability
-   Easier experimentation
-   A better understanding of what the technology actually requires

It also changes the relationship between human and machine.

The AI becomes a **compass, not a crutch**.

And, fittingly, I am currently using a local Qwen setup to proofread
this very post.

## Keep moving toward efficiency

I don't think the answer is to reject AI.

Quite the opposite.

We should keep researching and experimenting with it---but relentlessly
push toward **efficiency, transparency, sustainability, and user
agency**.

Smaller models. Better hardware. Better inference. Local execution. Open
weights. Better algorithms.

The goal should not be "how much of human thought can we outsource?"

The better question is:

> **"How much more can humans accomplish when the machine handles the
> parts machines are good at?"**

AI will change how we work.

It shouldn't change who we are.

**We are here to think better---not to think less.**

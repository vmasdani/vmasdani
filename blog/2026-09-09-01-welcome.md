# Welcome

This blog is a small corner of the internet for things I build, test,
learn, and think about.

The site itself follows the same philosophy. It is a starter **Laravel +
MySQL + shadcn** project, hosted on my own server, with a deliberately
simple, static-first architecture. There is a blog, and there are small
utilities such as online ping, Nmap, and IP/country checks. More useful
little tools will come as I think of them.

I am a pragmatist. I like technology when it makes things **simpler,
faster, cheaper, more reliable, or more capable**---not merely because
it is new.

That is why I am interested in efficient vehicles, efficient laptops,
and increasingly capable LLMs. I also care about **decentralization and
open-source software** because technology should give people agency
rather than quietly turning them into permanent dependents of a
platform.

You can probably understand me better by looking at the things I tend to
choose.

## What I like

### Programming

My preferred stack is intentionally boring where boring is useful:

1.  **Laravel / LAMP** --- mature, productive, easy to deploy, and pleasant to maintain.
2.  **TanStack** --- when the frontend genuinely benefits from a richer application architecture.

I don't need every project to use the trendiest stack. I want the stack
that gets the job done with the least unnecessary complexity.

### LLMs

I like small, fast, capable models and the direction toward running AI
closer to the user.

My current favorites include:

1.  **GLM 5.3 Flash**
2.  **Qwen 3.8 Flash**
3.  **DeepSeek V4.1 Flash**

The exact model matters less than the principle: **more useful
intelligence for less compute, money, latency, and dependency**.

### Cars

My favorites are the **Toyota Vios / Soluna** and **Daihatsu Xenia**.

They are not exotic. That is exactly the point. They are practical
machines with understandable engineering, abundant parts, and a strong
track record of simply working.

### Motorcycles

I have a particular soft spot for **Cubs**---Honda Wave/Supra, Super
Cub, Yamaha Vega, and similar machines.

I also think electric motorcycles are the future. But Indonesia's
biggest obstacle is not necessarily the battery. It is the
infrastructure around it: **repair shops, mechanics, and parts
availability across the streets**.

A technology is only efficient if the surrounding system is efficient
too.

## Why this blog exists

This is not meant to be a definitive technology publication. It is a
workshop.

I'll write about software, hardware, vehicles, AI, systems,
decentralization, and whatever project happens to be interesting at the
time.

The recurring question will be simple:

> **Can we make this better without making it unnecessarily
> complicated?**

That's the kind of question I like asking.
